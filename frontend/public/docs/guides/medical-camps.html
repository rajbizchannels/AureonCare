Medical Camps

Found under Scheduling → Medical Camps. Available on Clinical Pro and above.

Medical Camps supports time-boxed outreach events held at a venue your practice does not own — a school hall, a community centre, a partner site.

How a Camp Differs From a Clinic Day

A camp is not a slot calendar. Nobody books a 09:20 appointment. Instead:

  • Attendees arrive as walk-ins and are registered at a desk
  • They receive a queue token
  • They move through a pipeline of stations — registration, vitals, consultation, pharmacy
  • Each station has its own queue

A camp is a scheduling and reporting context laid over your normal clinical records. Notes, diagnoses, prescriptions and lab orders written at a camp live in the usual places — there is no second clinical system to reconcile afterwards.

Camp Planning

  1. Go to Scheduling → Medical Camps → Camp Planning
  2. Click + New Camp
  3. Enter the camp details
  4. Add the services the camp will offer
  5. Define the stations attendees pass through, in order
  6. Assign staff to stations
  7. List the supplies needed, drawn from Inventory where you track it
  8. Click Save

Camp Statuses

  • Draft — Being planned, not visible to the desk
  • Published — Plan agreed, staff and stations fixed
  • In Progress — Running; the desk and board are live
  • Completed — Finished; reporting available
  • Cancelled — Called off

Registration Desk

Built for speed under queue pressure.

  1. Enter name, phone number and age
  2. Capture consent
  3. Click Register
  4. A queue token is issued

A camp registration is deliberately not a patient record. The patient index requires an MRN and an exact date of birth; a registration desk has a name, a number and an approximate age. Keeping them separate is what lets the desk move quickly without filling your patient list with half-complete records.

Consent is enforced at the station too. An attendee without recorded consent cannot be started at a station, not just at the desk — so "we'll take the consent later" is not available under pressure.

Promoting a Registration to a Patient

When a camp attendee becomes an ongoing patient — a referral, a follow-up, a prescription to dispense later — promote their registration.

  1. Open the registration
  2. Click Promote to Patient
  3. The system shows existing patients who may be the same person, with phone-number matches ranked highest
  4. Either link to an existing patient, or create a new record
  5. Supply a date of birth

Promotion will not invent a date of birth. Enter the real one, or explicitly ask for an estimate — in which case the record is marked as holding an estimated date, so nobody later mistakes it for a verified one.

Tip: Promotion is the only bridge between camp registrations and your patient index, and it always shows possible duplicates first. That makes merging a decision someone made, rather than something that happened by accident at volume.

Station Board

A live view of the camp while it runs:

  • Every station, with its current queue length
  • Average and current waiting time per station
  • Which attendee is in progress at each station
  • Completed counts per station

Use it to spot a bottleneck and move staff while the camp is still running.

Working a Station

  1. Select your station
  2. Call the next token
  3. Click Start Visit — refused if consent was never recorded
  4. Deliver care and document it as you normally would
  5. Click Complete — the attendee moves to the next station

Camp Outcomes

Two distinct reports:

ReportForContains
Clinical reportThe practiceAttendees seen, conditions found, referrals, prescriptions, throughput per station and per hour
Sponsor reportThe funding organisationAggregate only, from an explicit list of permitted fields

Privacy: in the sponsor report, any category with fewer than five people is merged into a larger bucket. In a camp of eighty, a count of one plus local knowledge identifies somebody. Do not work around this by exporting the clinical report to a sponsor.

Exports: PDF, Excel and CSV.

Best Practices

  • ✓ Create the venue as a Location before planning the camp
  • ✓ Publish the camp before the day so the desk and board are ready
  • ✓ Order stations in the sequence attendees physically walk
  • ✓ Record consent at registration, every time
  • ✓ Promote registrations the same day, while staff still recall the attendee
  • ✓ Send sponsors the sponsor report, never the clinical one
  • ✗ Don't register attendees as full patients to "save time later"
  • ✗ Don't skip consent under queue pressure — the station will refuse the visit anyway
