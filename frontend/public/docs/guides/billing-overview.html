Revenue Cycle Management Overview

The Revenue Cycle Management (RCM) module manages the entire billing process from patient registration through final payment collection. It handles insurance claims, payment processing, payer management, and financial reporting.

What is Revenue Cycle Management?

Revenue Cycle Management is the financial process healthcare facilities use to track patient care episodes from registration and appointment scheduling to final payment. The RCM process involves:

  • Patient registration and insurance verification
  • Charge capture for services provided
  • Claims submission to insurance payers
  • Payment posting (insurance and patient)
  • Denial management and appeals
  • Patient billing and collections
  • Financial reporting and analysis

Accessing the RCM Module

  1. From the main navigation, click Revenue Cycle Management or RCM
  2. You'll see the RCM dashboard with key metrics:
    • Total revenue (current period)
    • Claims submitted
    • Claims pending
    • Claims denied
    • Outstanding A/R (accounts receivable)
    • Collection rate
  3. Use tabs to navigate to different sections:
    • Claims - Create and manage insurance claims
    • Payments - Record and track payments
    • Insurance Payers - Manage insurance companies
    • Reports - Financial and operational reports

Key RCM Workflows

1. Claims Management Workflow

Step Action Description
1 Create Claim Manually create or auto-generate from completed appointments
2 Add Diagnosis Add ICD-10 codes (up to 12)
3 Add Procedures Add CPT codes with charges
4 Verify Eligibility Check patient insurance coverage
5 Validate System checks for errors
6 Submit Electronic (EDI 837) or Paper (CMS-1500)
7 Track Status Monitor claim progress
8 Post Payment Apply insurance payment to claim

2. Payment Processing Workflow

  1. Receive Payment - Patient pays via cash, card, check, ACH, or patient portal
  2. Record Payment - Enter payment details in RCM > Payments
  3. Link to Claim/Invoice - Associate payment with specific claim or patient balance
  4. Post Payment - Apply payment to patient account
  5. Generate Receipt - Print or email receipt to patient
  6. Reconcile - Daily reconciliation of payments received

3. Denial Management Workflow

  1. Identify Denial - Monitor denied claims dashboard
  2. Review Denial Reason - Understand why claim was denied
  3. Determine Action - Correct and resubmit OR appeal
  4. Correct Errors - Fix diagnosis codes, procedures, or information
  5. Resubmit or Appeal - Send corrected claim or file appeal with documentation
  6. Track Resolution - Monitor resubmission/appeal status

Common Billing Tasks

Creating a Claim

  1. Go to RCM > Claims
  2. Click + New Claim
  3. Enter claim information:
    • Patient (search by name or MRN)
    • Insurance payer
    • Service date
    • Rendering provider
    • Place of service (e.g., Office: 11, Hospital: 21)
  4. Add diagnosis codes (ICD-10):
    • Primary diagnosis (required)
    • Secondary diagnoses (optional, up to 11 more)
  5. Add procedure codes (CPT):
    • CPT code
    • Description
    • Units (quantity)
    • Charge per unit
    • Total charge (auto-calculated)
  6. Review total claim amount
  7. Click Save Claim

Recording a Payment

  1. Go to RCM > Payments
  2. Click + New Payment
  3. Select patient
  4. Enter payment amount
  5. Select payment method:
    • Credit Card - Enter last 4 digits, card type, transaction ID
    • Cash - Record amount received
    • Check - Enter check number, bank name
    • ACH/Bank Transfer - Enter transaction reference
    • Insurance Payment - Link to claim
  6. Select payment type:
    • Copay
    • Coinsurance
    • Deductible
    • Self-Pay (full payment)
  7. Link to claim or invoice (optional)
  8. Add description/notes
  9. Click Save Payment
  10. Print or email receipt to patient

Managing Insurance Payers

  1. Go to RCM > Insurance Payers
  2. View all insurance companies
  3. Click + Add Insurance Payer to add new
  4. Enter payer information:
    • Payer name
    • Payer ID (electronic)
    • Address and contact information
    • Claims submission method (Electronic/Paper)
    • Claims address (for paper claims)
    • Contact person
    • Notes (special requirements)
  5. Configure payer-specific settings:
    • Prior authorization requirements
    • Accepted procedure codes
    • Fee schedule
    • Expected payment timeframe
  6. Click Save Payer

Common Denial Reasons and Solutions

Denial Reason Explanation Solution
Missing Information Required fields not completed Add missing diagnosis, procedure, or demographic info
Invalid Code ICD-10 or CPT code incorrect Verify codes are current and valid
Not Covered Service Service not covered by plan Bill patient as self-pay or appeal with medical necessity
Pre-Auth Required Prior authorization needed Obtain authorization, add auth number to claim

Best Practices for Revenue Cycle Management

Claim Submission Best Practices

  • ✅ Verify insurance before every appointment
  • ✅ Collect copays at time of service
  • ✅ Submit claims within 24-48 hours of service
  • ✅ Use electronic submission for faster processing (EDI 837)
  • ✅ Validate claims before submission to catch errors
  • ✅ Include all diagnosis codes to support medical necessity
  • ✅ Use specific CPT codes rather than generic codes
  • ✅ Link diagnosis to procedures to show medical necessity

Denial Management Best Practices

  • ✅ Review denials daily - Address within 24 hours
  • ✅ Track denial reasons - Identify patterns
  • ✅ Correct and resubmit quickly - Within 7-10 days
  • ✅ Appeal when appropriate - With supporting documentation
  • ✅ Document denial trends - Train staff on prevention
  • ✅ Follow up on appeals - Don't let them expire

Payment Posting Best Practices

  • ✅ Post payments daily - Don't let them accumulate
  • ✅ Reconcile daily deposits - Match to payment records
  • ✅ Generate receipts - For all patient payments
  • ✅ Document payment method - For audit trail
  • ✅ Apply to correct claim - Link payments accurately
  • ✅ Handle refunds promptly - Process within 30 days

Financial Reporting Best Practices

  • ✅ Review reports weekly - Monitor key metrics
  • ✅ Track Days in A/R - Keep under 40 days
  • ✅ Monitor collection rate - Maintain above 95%
  • ✅ Review aging reports - Follow up on old balances
  • ✅ Analyze denial trends - Implement corrective actions
  • ✅ Compare period over period - Identify revenue trends

Common Questions

Q: How quickly should claims be submitted?

A: Best practice is to submit claims within 24-48 hours of service. Most payers have filing deadlines (30-90 days), but faster submission leads to faster payment.

Q: What if a patient doesn't have insurance?

A: Mark the patient as self-pay and collect payment at time of service or offer a payment plan. Do not submit claims to insurance payers.

Q: How do I handle partial insurance payments?

A: Post the insurance payment to the claim, then bill the patient for the remaining balance (deductible, coinsurance, or copay). Generate a patient statement.

Q: Can I edit a claim after it's submitted?

A: No, submitted claims cannot be edited. If you need to make changes, you must void the original claim and create a corrected claim.

Q: How long should we wait before following up on a claim?

A: Follow up if no response within:

  • Electronic claims: 14 days
  • Paper claims: 21 days

Q: What's the difference between copay, coinsurance, and deductible?

A:

  • Copay: Fixed amount patient pays per visit (e.g., $25)
  • Coinsurance: Percentage patient pays after deductible (e.g., 20%)
  • Deductible: Amount patient must pay before insurance coverage begins (e.g., $1,000/year)
