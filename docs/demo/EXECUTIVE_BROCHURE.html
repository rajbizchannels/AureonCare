MedFlow Prepared for the Chief Medical Officer and the Dean

One patient record, from the booking to the paid claim.

MedFlow brings scheduling, the clinical record, telehealth, e-prescribing, lab orders and billing into one ambulatory platform built on FHIR R4. This brief covers what it does today, where it is not yet ready for an academic medical center, and a pilot that would show whether it earns a place here.

The platform

Six modules, one record

Each module reads and writes the same patient record. A diagnosis entered during a visit is the same diagnosis the billing team codes and the same condition a lab order is linked to.

Scheduling

Appointment types and durations, provider availability and time off, a waitlist, and reminders by SMS, email or WhatsApp.

Clinical record

Diagnoses with ICD-10 search, allergies, medication history, prescriptions, and visit records with attached documents.

Telehealth

Video visits started from the appointment itself, through Zoom, Google Meet or Webex.

Prescriptions and labs

Prescriptions routed through Surescripts to the patient's preferred pharmacy, with drug interaction data. Lab orders sent to Labcorp. Each order gets a tracking number such as LAB-123456 and a status timeline.

Revenue cycle

Claims with EDI 837 generation, submission through the Optum clearinghouse, payment posting, denials and appeals, and prior authorizations.

Interoperability

FHIR R4 resources for Patient, Condition, MedicationRequest, ServiceRequest and Observation. A patient portal in eight languages.

Vendor connections to Surescripts, Labcorp and Optum are built with sandbox and production modes. Production use requires contracts and credentialing with each vendor.

For leadership

What each office should know

Chief Medical Officer

What it offers clinical leadership

  • A single longitudinal chart across scheduling, visits, prescriptions, labs and billing.
  • Allergies and current medications in view when prescribing, with drug interaction data behind e-prescribing.
  • Every prescription and lab order is tracked, so a result that never came back is visible.
  • Telehealth visits use the same record and documentation as in-person visits.

What to know before live use

  • A saved clinical note can be edited or deleted later, and no prior version is kept.
  • There is no log of who opened a patient's chart.
  • Clinical quality measures, such as HbA1c testing or blood pressure control rates, are not available. Current reports cover operations and revenue only.
  • The "no-show rate" in today's reports counts cancellations. It should be corrected before anyone relies on it.

Dean

Where it fits today

  • Faculty ambulatory practices that do not involve trainees.
  • Telehealth for rural and outreach clinics, documented in the same record.
  • Records stored as standard FHIR R4 resources, which a future research export could draw on.

What the academic mission needs that it lacks

  • No distinction between residents, fellows and attendings. There is one physician role.
  • No attending co-signature or attestation, which supervised trainee documentation and teaching-physician billing require.
  • No research cohort tools or de-identified data export.
  • No separation by site or department. A tenant field exists but no query uses it.

Proposed path

Prove it before relying on it

Phase 0

Close the security gaps

Real authentication on every function, chart-access logging and locked notes, followed by an independent security assessment.

Phase 1

Sandbox pilot

One non-teaching faculty clinic runs its workflows on synthetic patients against vendor sandboxes. No real patient data.

Phase 2

Limited live pilot

Real patients in that clinic, only after compliance and information security sign off on Phase 0.

Phase 3

Teaching features

Trainee roles, co-signature and attestation designed with Graduate Medical Education, then piloted in one residency clinic.

What the pilot measures, against the clinic's current baseline
  • Time to register a new patient
  • Time from visit end to completed note
  • Prescriptions and lab orders that needed a phone call or fax
  • Lab results that returned to the chart without manual entry
  • Claims accepted on first submission
  • Actual no-shows, counted separately from cancellations

This brief gives no outcome figures. Any savings or efficiency numbers should come from the pilot's own measurements.

What we are asking for

  1. Joint sponsorship of a Phase 0 and Phase 1 scoping review by the CMO and the Dean.
  2. A physician lead from a non-teaching faculty clinic to host the sandbox pilot.
  3. Representatives from Graduate Medical Education and Information Security to set the conditions for Phases 2 and 3.
