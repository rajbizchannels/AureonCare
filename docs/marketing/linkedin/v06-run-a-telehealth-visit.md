```
Most practices did telehealth the fast way, because in 2020 there was no slow way. Someone opened a video tool, pasted a link into an email, and the clinic ran virtual visits on a workflow that lived entirely outside the patient record.

Five years on, a lot of clinics are still doing exactly that — and paying for it in small, invisible ways. The link that went to the wrong patient. The consent form signed in the first two minutes of the consultation instead of before it. The visit that happened but left no trace in the chart.

This issue is about running a virtual visit from inside the record instead of alongside it. Watch it first, it is just over two minutes:

[ VIDEO 6 LINK ]
```

```
The session is built from the booking

Clinical ▸ Telehealth is where virtual visits live. A green banner at the top names the video platform the practice has connected — in the demo that is Google Meet.

Two things follow from that banner being there at all.

The first is that your administrator connects that account once, centrally. Clinicians never touch credentials, never sign into a shared account, and never have to remember whose personal video room the Tuesday clinic uses.

The second is that a telehealth session is not created from scratch. It is built from a booked appointment, so the patient, the provider and the time are already right — they came from the calendar, not from someone retyping them. The commonest telehealth error in practices running this on email is sending a link for the wrong time or to the wrong person. That error is not available here, because nobody is typing those three things twice.

AureonCare confirms what it is about to do, then creates the meeting.
```

**→ Insert `assets/v06/03-consent-before.png` here.**

```
Consent arrives before the call, not during it

This is the part worth changing your process for.

When you create the session you can attach a consent form, and it goes to the patient's portal ahead of the appointment. They read it and sign it in their own time, at home, without a clinician on screen waiting.

Compare that with the usual version, where consent is handled in the first two minutes of the consultation. That costs you twice. It burns consultation time you booked for clinical work, and it collects consent from someone who is being watched while they read — which is the worst possible condition for actually reading it.

Sending it ahead is better for the schedule and better for the patient. Those two things do not often point the same way.
```

**→ Insert `assets/v06/04-two-ways-in.png` here.**

```
Two ways into a call

Once created, the visit sits under Upcoming Sessions, tagged with the platform and carrying its join link. Clicking Join marks the session in progress — so the rest of the team can see you are in it rather than wondering whether to interrupt — and the meeting room opens in a new tab.

That covers the planned visit. For everything else there is Instant, which creates a room immediately with no booking behind it.

Use it for what it is for: the unplanned. A patient who calls in and needs to be seen now, a colleague who needs a second opinion on something in front of them, a follow-up that turns out to be two minutes rather than twenty. The scheduled path exists so the record stays tidy; Instant exists so that tidiness never becomes the reason someone does not get seen.
```
